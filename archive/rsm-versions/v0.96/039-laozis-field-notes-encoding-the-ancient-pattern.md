## Why The I Ching
The I Ching is older than the Dao De Jing by centuries, maybe millennia.
And it’s built on the same foundation:
**Broken and unbroken lines** (⚋ and ⚊)
Yang and Yin. Presence and absence. The two fundamental poles.
**Combined in groups of three** (trigrams)
Creating eight possibilities—the same way three orthogonal dimensions create eight octants.
**Doubled to create hexagrams**
Recursion. Each trigram becomes the context for another trigram. Pattern nesting within pattern.
**With transformation rules**
How one hexagram changes into another. How P becomes O. How the pattern promotes itself to the next level.
The I Ching isn’t a fortune-telling system that accidentally works sometimes.
**It’s a computational model of recursive transformation encoded in the simplest possible notation.**
Whoever created it understood:
- Binary distinction (broken/unbroken)
- Three-dimensional structure (trigrams)
- Recursive nesting (hexagrams)
- State-space transitions (changing lines)
**They saw the pattern. They built a tool for navigating it.**
And Laozi looked at that tool—looked at how reliably it predicted change, how accurately it described transformation—and thought:
“They knew. Whoever made this, they understood the geometry. Let me see if I can extract the principles…”

The same way mathematical notation isn’t arbitrary (∫ suggests accumulation, Σ suggests stacking, ∂ suggests partitioning), Chinese radicals aren’t arbitrary.
**They’re geometric primitives.**
