The Recursive Geometry of Arboreal Form
A tree has a pith.
The pith is the structural center of the
trunk. The living tissue surrounds it but
does not occupy it. The pith is Pₙ: the
required-and-forbidden center at the
trunk's scale.
The annual rings are concentric circles
around the pith, each a complete circuit
of the trunk's growth in one season.
Each ring is S¹ at the trunk's scale,
centered on Pₙ.
The cambium is the active growth boundary:
the outermost ring of living cells,
generating new wood inward and new bark
outward. It is the frame's operative
gradient — the zone where the two modes
(heartwood-direction and sapwood-direction)
are actively co-obtaining.
At a specific point on the cambium, a
branch initiates. That point is a position
on the parent ring: a position on Gₙ.
At that position:
The trunk's pith axis (Pₙ) generates
a branch origin (O₍ₙ₊₁₎).
The branch develops its own pith: a new
structural center around which its own
rings will grow.
The branch's rings are perpendicular to
the branch's axis, not the trunk's axis.
A new coordinate system. Not a new
dimension — the same three-dimensional
space, a new orientation within it.
In the 常 register:
The branch axis is perfectly perpendicular
to the trunk axis. The branching angle is
90°. The exchange-symmetry of the trunk's
gradient determines the branch's departure
direction.
In the 可 register:
The actual branch angle is determined by
the operating gradients: gravity pulling
the branch toward horizontal, light
pulling it toward vertical, competition
with adjacent branches pushing it into
available space. No two branches depart
at exactly the same angle. Each is a
可-level expression of the 常 structural
pattern.
The knot is the visible record of the
branching event in the wood. Where the
trunk's ring structure is interrupted by
the branch's departure, the grain curves
around the branching point. The knot is
Pₙ → O₍ₙ₊₁₎ made permanently visible in
the wood's structure.
The branch follows the same logic as the
trunk:
Its own pith (its own Pₙ₊₁).
Its own concentric rings (its own S¹
at the branch's scale).
Its own cambium (its own active growth
boundary).
Its own potential branching points where
sub-branches can initiate (its own
Pₙ₊₁ → O₍ₙ₊₂₎).
The recursion goes both ways:
Downward: every branch can generate
sub-branches from its own cambium,
with the same structural logic at
finer scale. No smallest branch.
Upward: the trunk is itself a branch
of the root system, which has its own
pith and ring structure oriented along
its own axis. The root's pith is the
coarser frame's Pₙ₋₁. The trunk's pith
is that root's O₍ₙ₎. The recursion
continues downward into the soil.
All share an original pith:
The seed's first axis, from which the
primary root extended downward and the
primary shoot extended upward. Every
pith in the tree — trunk, branch, twig,
root — was generated from that first
structural origin through the same
recursive logic.
The locally flat / globally curved dynamic:
At the branch's scale: the branch's rings
look like concentric circles around the
branch's own axis. Locally flat.
From the trunk's perspective: the branch
is a curved departure from the trunk's
ring structure. Globally curved.
The branch does not know it is curved.
It only knows its own axis.
Reading the record:
Annual rings disclose time: each ring is
one year's growth. The width of the ring
discloses the growing conditions that
year — wide rings in good years, narrow
rings in poor years.
The grain pattern discloses the branching
history: every knot is a branch origin,
every grain curve is a record of the
gradient the living tissue navigated
around a branching point.
Charring discloses the density gradient:
soft earlywood (grown in spring, less
dense) burns faster than dense latewood
(grown in summer, more dense), making
the annual ring structure topographically
visible.
Different instruments disclose different
aspects of the same underlying structure.
The tree is not an illustration of the
RSM. The tree is a physical instantiation
of the structural pattern, operating at
its own scale under its own 可-level
gradients, leaving its own record in
wood.
