## 1. Looking at a Tree (木, 母, 季, 子)

When you look at an oak or pine tree, habit treats it as a solid wooden post sitting inside an
empty box called "space." In reality, the tree is a circulating hydraulic boundary maintaining a
local distinction ($1_{\mathrm{tree}} \neq 0_{\mathrm{tree}}$) between soil water and sky:

**The Living Seam (Gₙ):** Neither the dead heartwood at the center nor the rough outer bark is
dividing. All generation happens in a microscopic cylindrical ring — the vascular cambium — which
lays down water-carrying xylem inward and sugar-carrying phloem outward.

**Three Ways a Frame Continues:**

1. **Radial Rings (Staying in $R_n$):** Expanding the trunk's circumference along the existing
   gradient.
2. **Branching (Nested Recursion $\Phi_n(P_n) = O_{n+1}$):** At a node on the cambial ring
   ($\mathcal{P}_{n,y} \cong S^1$), the wood grain twists into an orthogonal torus-knot and shoots
   outward at an angle. Spaced around the trunk by the Golden Angle
   ($2\pi/\varphi^2 \approx 137.5^\circ$) so upper limbs never directly shade lower ones and knots
   never align to split the trunk, what was a localized pinch ($P_n$) on the parent becomes the
   central axis ($O_{n+1}$) of a child limb.
3. **The Seed (季 = 禾+子 $\to$ standalone 子):** The tree packages its entire relational code into
   an acorn and drops it onto the forest floor. Disconnected from the parent's plumbing, 99% of
   acorns rot back into humus as "straw dogs" (芻狗), nourishing the soil; one that catches a
   gradient unfurls root and shoot (子 popping open its horizontal arms) as an independent tree.
   And when the old tree finally dies, it does not fall out of Reality; it simply ceases to
   maintain $1_{\mathrm{tree}} \neq 0_{\mathrm{tree}}$ and returns its timber to the circulation of
   the forest.

## 2. The Water-Carrier on the Mountain Switchback (扁擔 and the Two Chu Glyphs for 道)

Look at the two ways the Guodian Laozi draws 道:

- In one version (行), it draws the pure four-stroke hyperbolic intersection — the map of the
  crossroads from above.
- In the other version (道), it draws a person striding off-frame to the left, holding a bamboo
  pole across their shoulder, with two strings tied to a rounded water bucket (marked with two
  interior dashes to show its curvature), standing right above the zigzag mountain switchback path
  they just walked down.

Think about the physical reality of carrying water down a steep mountain trail in Chu or Shu:

**Why You Cannot Walk the Straight Line ($x = y$):** If you try to march straight down the vertical
fall-line of a steep mountain carrying heavy water buckets on an iron bar, gravity accelerates you
into an uncontrollable crash, and every step slams the water out of the buckets.

**The Zigzag Switchback (辶/止 at the bottom of 道):** Instead of plunging straight through the
center, the trail cuts across the slope to the left ($G_n^+$), reaches a hairpin turn
(返也者，道之動也), and cuts back across the slope to the right ($G_n^-$). The switchback turns an
impossible vertical drop into a navigable reciprocal gradient.

**The Wobble of the Bamboo Pole (弱也者，道之用也):** Suspended by two ropes from the split green
bamboo pole across your shoulder, the rounded bucket rocks in a gentle arc. As your foot strikes
the trail, the bamboo flexes downward to absorb the shock; as you stride forward, the pole rebounds
upward out of phase with the sloshing water, keeping the bucket full. Everywhere you stand on that
switchback is an intersection ($(1,1)$), because your own shoulder is the moving hinge where the
vertical pull of gravity and the horizontal cut of the trail balance each other.

## 3. Dujiangyan (谷, 浴, 浧): Designing with the Unoccupied Channel

Built around 256 BCE in the same river-and-mountain world as the Guodian slips, the Dujiangyan
irrigation system tamed the raging Min River without building a rigid wall across the current
(為之者敗之，執者失之 — Slip A.12). Li Bing shaped the riverbed using the exact three-step geometry
drawn in the Chu glyph for Valley (谷):

1. **Dividing (魚嘴 Fish Mouth :: the upper split 八/八 of 谷):** A low permeable gravel island
   splits the river into an outer floodway and an inner irrigation canal (非 — two wings diverging
   from one seam).
2. **Settling & Centrifuging (飛沙堰 Flying Sand Weir :: 浧, Slip A.16.17):** As the water bends
   through a curved basin (口/皿), helical vortex circulation flings 80% of the heavy boulders and
   clumped silt (天坨, Slip A.18.18) up and over a low spillway lip back into the outer river,
   leaving the inner water clear (徐清).
3. **Metering (寶瓶口 Bottle-Neck Mouth :: the bottom 口 of 谷 and 浴):** A narrow throat cut
   straight through the mountain rock meters a steady flow onto the Chengdu plain (川, Slip A.3.31)
   while automatically backing up excess floodwater to spill over the weir.
