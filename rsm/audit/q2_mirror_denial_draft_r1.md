## In plain terms first

The chains left open a question: the gradient's level set falls into two pieces, and nobody had ruled
whether that means **two things**, or **one thing the drawing shows twice**.

The "one thing shown twice" answer needs the two pieces to be exact mirrors of each other. Your
ruling is that a perfect mirror can't obtain, for the same reason exact balance can't: a mirror-claim
is a claim about *every* level of detail, and the levels don't run out. You never get to the bottom
and confirm it. So the mirror is never established — not disproven, never established.

That leaves the pieces genuinely two. But they aren't unrelated: each stands in one relation to a
shared center — the center sits exactly between them. That's a different kind of claim. It isn't a
comparison between the two pieces at all; it's one relation each has to a third thing. Nothing there
has to be checked at every level, so the depth argument doesn't bite.

**One correction to how it was stated.** You put it as "equidistant from the shared center," with the
circumference as all such points. Distance needs the measure, and v9 hasn't earned the measure — it's
`[unaudited]`. But the argument doesn't need distance. "The center sits exactly between them" is a
statement about **midpoint**, which survives without any measure at all. I checked: shear a figure so
that all distances change, and midpoints are still midpoints. So the argument works in the skeleton
where you need it; the circle arrives later, in the layer that has a measure. **Confirm that
restatement says what you meant** — it's your ruling, in different words.

## The claim

> **Claim.** The two components of `{Q_j = 1ₙ}` are genuinely two. The second is not the chart's
> image of the first. Their relation is **central** (each bears one relation to a shared center), not
> **reflective** (a correspondence between them).

## Premises and prior results used

| | |
|---|---|
| **P1 — the Conditional** | no terminal resolution in either direction; in particular **no floor inward** |
| **The Corollary** | every distinction constitutes a frame; distinction beneath a frame's unit is *another frame* |
| **v9 structural §5** | "equality carried through every depth is a terminal resolution, and P1 denies terminals" |
| **T1** *(verified topology)* | `{Q_j = 1ₙ}` has two components; `Pₙ` and `ν(Pₙ)` lie in different ones; no path within the slice joins them |

Not used: the measure, the metric, positive-definiteness, the circle. The argument is affine
throughout except where marked **[explicit layer]**.

## The argument

**S1 — What the horn requires.** T1's open interpretation offers: *the second component is the
chart's image of the first.* For that to hold, the two must stand in an **exact** correspondence —
otherwise they differ, and differing is the other horn. Call this assertion the **mirror claim**.

**S2 — What the mirror claim asserts.** That every distinction obtaining on one component has an
exact counterpart on the other. It is a claim *about the two objects, compared to each other*.

**S3 — Distinctions do not bottom out.** By P1 there is no floor inward. By the Corollary, a
distinction beneath a frame's unit is not a finer reading within that frame but *another frame*.
So beneath each component lies a lineage of frames that does not terminate. *[derived: P1 + Corollary]*

**S4 — Therefore the mirror claim is a claim about every depth.** The correspondence of S2 must hold
through the whole non-terminating lineage of S3. It is an exact correspondence required at every
level, indexed by a descent that does not finish. *[derived: S2 + S3]*

**S5 — Such a claim is a terminal resolution, which P1 denies.** This is the same denial v9 already
applies to exact balance. The mirror is **not falsified** — no asymmetry need be exhibited — it is
**never established**, because the descent never completes.
***[Will's ruling, 2026-07-27, not derived here: "it's the same argument. two points can't mirror
BECAUSE you think you have a mirror but then can always zoom in to find further distinction."]***

**S6 — The horn fails.** The mirror claim cannot obtain, so the second component cannot be
established as the chart's image of the first. **The components are two.** *[derived: S1 + S5]*

**S7 — What survives, and why it is a different kind of object.** The two loci can stand in an exact
relation that is **not** a correspondence between them: `Oₙ` is the **midpoint** of `Pₙ` and
`ν(Pₙ)`. Each bears *one* relation to a shared *third* term. This does not quantify over either
object's internal distinctions, so S3 gives it nothing to descend into and S5 does not apply.

> **Why the exemption is principled and not special pleading.** The relation's third term is `Oₙ`,
> which v9 types as *"a locus, never a position; nothing stands there."* An interiorless term has no
> lineage beneath it for a correspondence to be required through. The exemption rests on `Oₙ`'s
> unmet status — stated here rather than assumed.

**S8 — The relation is affine, hence skeleton-available.** *[checked]* Midpoint is preserved by
affine maps that do not preserve distance: under a shear, two loci antipodal about `Oₙ` keep `Oₙ` as
their exact midpoint while their distances from it change (1.000 → 2.117 in the worked case). So the
central relation needs **no measure** and is available in the implicit skeleton, before the explicit
layer exists.

**S9 — The circle is the explicit-layer rendering, not a skeleton posit.** *[explicit layer]* "All
points equidistant from the shared center" is the circumference, and equidistance is metric. Once the
measure is in force, the two loci are poles of a diameter and the circle is what that phrase names —
not something posited alongside it. But this arrives **with** the measure, which v9 tags
definitional-by-register and `[unaudited]`. **The conclusion must not be stated circle-first in the
skeleton.**

**Conclusion.** The components are two, and their relation is central, not reflective. At skeleton
level the binding is the midpoint relation through `Oₙ`. Its explicit-layer rendering is the orbit —
T1's *door*. *[derived, given S5]*

## The weakest joints — targets for the adversarial read

**W1 — the chart-generation objection. This is the weakest joint.** S1 assumes the "chart's image"
horn requires an exact correspondence to be *established*. An opponent can say: if the chart
*generates* the second component, nothing needs checking — the drawing procedure guarantees the
match, the way a reflection in a drawing needs no verification. The reply is that T1's two-ness is
topology of the level set, which is the law's own object rather than the drawing's. But the level set
is written in coordinates, and whether "two components" is chart-independent is close to the very
question at issue. **This argument assumes it is. That assumption should be attacked first.**

**W2 — S5 is a ruling, not a derivation.** The step from "an exact correspondence indexed by a
non-terminating descent" to "a terminal resolution P1 denies" is Will's ruling that the two are the
same argument. It is recorded, not proved. An auditor should treat it as a named premise-level move
and ask whether the two objects are the same kind of thing. *(Answering that is what the ruling did;
the point is that it be visible as a ruling.)*

**W3 — S7's exemption leans on `Oₙ`.** The central relation escapes the depth argument because its
third term is interiorless. If `Oₙ` turned out to have structure beneath it, the exemption would
weaken. v9 says it does not — *"nothing stands there"* — but the dependency should be visible.

**W4 — the restatement is mine.** The ruling was given in metric language (equidistant, circumference);
the write-up recasts its core as midpoint, which is affine. That strengthens it — the argument then
lives in the skeleton — but the words are not the ruler's. **Requires confirmation.**
