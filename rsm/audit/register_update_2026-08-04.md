### A-n9. φ as a location on the gradient
**OFFERED / CHECKABLE.** Read the 1 in φ's defining relation as 1ₙ. On XY = 1ₙ, the
frame's own length unit is √1ₙ (P sits at (√1ₙ, √1ₙ)). Solve XY = 1ₙ together with
X − Y = √1ₙ: **X = √1ₙ·φ, Y = √1ₙ/φ.**

So φ is not a constant assigned to anything — it is the **location on the gradient where
the mode-difference equals the frame's own unit**, with the conjugates there being φ and
1/φ (the product law with the unit factored out). And the same equation that locates it,
φ = 1 + 1/φ, is the one that iterates into the all-1s continued fraction — i.e. the
location condition and the worst-approximability condition are **one equation**.

*This supersedes nothing in the chains* (φ appears in none of them) but it replaces the
retired e/φ architecture's motivation with something checkable.
*Status:* editorial. Strong candidate for the Euler paper's next revision or its own
paper; no chain target proposed.

### A-n13. The conjugate-pair polynomial; the pentagon as the law one level up
**OFFERED / CHECKABLE.** Every unit-circle seat-pair (e^{iθ}, e^{−iθ}) satisfies
**z² − 2cos θ·z + 1 = 0**: product of roots = 1 (the circle invariant, Q_i), sum of
roots = 2cos θ = twice the split coordinate a. One quadratic carrying both slices —
product fixed, sum varying along the gradient coordinate. Euler's identity is this
family at θ = π (pair-sum −2). The corpus's "Master Identity"
(e^{2iπ/5} − φ·e^{iπ/5} + 1 = 0, verified: it is 2cos(π/5) = φ as a quadratic) is the
same family at θ = π/5 (pair-sum φ). **Not two registers — two seats of one law.**

**Why five:** the tenth-turn pair sums to φ; the fifth-turn pair sums to 1/φ; and the
two sums themselves satisfy **product 1, difference 1** — A-n9's location equation with
1ₙ = 1. The frame law re-instantiates among its own pair-sums. Self-similarity falls
out as the law reappearing one level up rather than being postulated (the old
"frame-invariance" derivation of φ was the retired fractal postulate). Four gives sums
{0, ±√2}; six gives {±1, ±2}; five is where it closes on itself.

*Supersedes* the Master Identity's register framing (Euler :: 可道 / Master :: 常道 —
the R8 type error, both retired; no equation is 常-register). *Falsifies in place:* the
doc's claim that it is "the only equation relating all six constants with coefficients
from {−1, 0, 1}" — its middle coefficient is −φ.
*Anachronism flag:* the 五行/五生變化 framing in those documents must not enter DDJ
material — the Guodian tomb's genuine 五行 manuscript is a separate Zisi-school text,
not the Laozi.
*Status:* editorial; candidate for the Euler paper's next revision or a companion
paper. No chain target.

### A-n12. The two horns have different closure behavior
**RULED (Will) — the organizing result of the session's late run.** Which horn of A-n5 a
structure takes determines whether closure is available to it:

- **Boundary held, center varying.** The reference is the boundary; closure is asked of
  the boundary and can be granted. Exact repetition is available — at the cost of
  maintenance. This is the crystal, and it is why a crystal cannot respond: a structure
  that has closed has nothing open left to respond with. Same shape as the tomb
  (`paper_ch11`): completion, and completion's price.
- **Center held, boundary varying.** The reference is the invariant. Closure would
  require the varying to return exactly to a prior state — fixing both ends at once,
  the excluded case. **A held center therefore forces quasiperiodicity**, not as a
  limitation but as the mode of maintenance. Tree, orbit, native protein state,
  escapement.

*Consequence:* periodicity and quasiperiodicity are not two points on one scale. They
are the two horns' respective closure behaviors, and φ (A-n10) is extremal **within the
second horn only.**
*Consequence for the tree-runs-both finding (B-n4):* it stops being an observation and
becomes structural — rings are boundary-held (one per year, countable, closed);
branching is center-held (pith invariant, each branch a new orientation, never
repeating). DNA likewise: the backbone repeats its unit (boundary-held, periodic) while
the rungs never repeat an orientation (center-held, quasiperiodic). One structure, two
references, both horns.
*Status:* **PATCHABLE, but the target needs a ruling** — this is arguably structural
(it follows from the exactness exclusion) rather than editorial. Recommend it be drafted
for the structural chain and ruled there, not slipped in as a physics correspondence.

**OFFERED / CHECKABLE.** Two masses orbit a barycenter neither occupies, always

## Section C — open formal work

### C-n1. What excludes chaos in three dimensions
**OPEN — recovered from the project's own March 2026 critique, never answered.**
Poincaré–Bendixson forces trajectories in continuous 2D systems to fixed points or limit
cycles; fixed points violate the unoccupiable center, hence cycles. But the theorem does
not hold in 3D, and the framework uses it to escape 2D sterility and then moves to 3D,
where chaos is mathematically permitted. Strange attractors are bounded, recurrent,
structurally stable, and chaotic.

**Historical note, new this session:** this is *the three-body problem*. Poincaré's 1890
work on it is the origin of the question, so "answering it" means engaging that
literature rather than deriving around it.

**The honest reply, incomplete in the corpus:** KAM theory plus Hurwitz — orbits survive
perturbation in proportion to how badly their frequency ratio approximates rationals,
with φ extremal (A-n9, A-n10). That reframes the question from *"is chaos excluded?"* to
*"quasiperiodic orbits occupy positive measure, indexed by distance from
commensurability."* Different type of answer than the corpus was seeking, and possibly
the right one.
*Closes with:* a formalization of the closure requirement, or a recorded weakening.

### C-n2. π's role, stated precisely
**Carried from the same critique, so it is not re-derived.** The non-repetition arises
in the incommensurability of around and through — **not in the orbit**; a circle is a
closed curve that does repeat. The Euler paper's step/seat distinction is the answer: the
**step** does not close; the **seats** do.
