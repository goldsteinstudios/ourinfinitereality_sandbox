## Verdict, first

**Will's instinct is supported.** For the macro-changes in the DDJ's transmission — the graph
substitutions, the 恆→常 merger, the 也-stripping, the fixing of one reading per graph — the
historiography and paleography point to **systemic, directional, largely deliberate processes**
(script reform, clerical transformation, taboo policy, editorial collation, metrical
regularization), not to accumulated copyist accident. The "one copyist at a time" picture is not
just unsupported for this period; the field has argued *against* it as a model of how these texts
existed at all.

Scribal error survives as a **local** mechanism — real for individual token variants, and it must
stay listed as a threat where the environment invites it (a repeated 有 is still an eye-skip
environment). But it is the minority mechanism, invoked case-by-case, not the engine of the
Guodian → Mawangdui → received drift.

One guard, stated now: the strong form of the framing — a Chu glyph as shorthand ***meant*** *to
open a lesson's worth of meaning* — is an **intentionality claim with no witness**. What the
paleography attests is the weaker form, which is enough: the graphs *underdetermined* their
words, reading was an act of resolution, and standardization removed the latitude. "The depth was
there and standardization removed it" is defensible; "the depth was put there on purpose" is the
kind of claim the old etymology guard existed to catch. Details in §4.

## 1. The Qin consolidation — real direction, wrong date, wrong agent

The traditional story (Shiji, Hanshu, Shuowen postface): after 221 BCE, Li Si's 書同文字 policy
abolished the regional scripts, standardized small seal, and made writing uniform.

**Imre Galambos's "The Myth of the Qin Unification of Writing in Han Sources" (Acta Orientalia
57, 2004)** re-examined those three sources and the archaeology and found: the archaeological
material before and after the First Emperor "does not reflect this vision of a sudden and
comprehensive shift in writing." Orthographic consistency as an *idea* "was most likely not a Qin
invention but a retrospective view from the Eastern Han dynasty nearly three centuries later,"
with a political agenda behind ascribing it to a single victorious ruler. Xu Shen's small seal
headings in the Shuowen (c. 100 CE) often do not even match actual Qin seal forms — the Shuowen's
"Qin standard" is partly a Han reconstruction.

**What this does to the framing:** it *strengthens* it. The consolidation was not one decree but
a **multi-century hardening running through exactly the window our three witnesses span** —
Guodian (~300 BCE, before), Mawangdui (~168 BCE, inside), received text (after, fixed under Han
classicism). And the agent was substantially **Han scholarship** — the same institution that
produced the received text. The hardening and the canonization were one process, done by the same
hands. A naive "Qin hardened the script" phrasing should be avoided; "Qin-through-Han
consolidation" is the defensible form.

## 2. 隸變 (the clerical transformation) — the flattening has a name and a mechanism

The seal-to-clerical transition (libian, mostly 3rd–2nd c. BCE) is described in the paleography
as "a fundamental reconfiguration of the structural principles underlying character formation" —
not a copying process at all, but a rewriting of the graphs themselves for administrative speed:

- **Omission** — components deleted outright (書 lost its phonetic 者);
- **Modulation** — components replaced by unrelated ones (射 acquiring 身);
- **Mutation** — forms altered until "no clue of the original form remained" (春);
- *(background)* **Convergence** — distinct seal components collapsing into identical clerical
  strokes, so that unrelated structures become graphically indistinguishable.

This is the concrete mechanism by which **radical-level structure stopped being readable**. A
Chu-script graph wears its composition; a clerical graph frequently does not, and sometimes the
composition it appears to wear is false. Whatever a graph's components were doing —
semantically motivated or not — libian ended the reader's access to it. This loss was
**systemic and irreversible**, and no copyist erred to produce it.

Note the reflexive point for this project: radical-level analysis (the project's primary
instrument, per the v9.3 etymology-guard removal) is only possible *against pre-clerical forms* —
which is precisely why the Guodian strips matter and the received graphs cannot substitute.

## 3. What variation was, before the hardening — flexibility, not error

The manuscript-studies literature is close to consensus on this:

- **Boltz** (CSMC Hamburg): reading an early manuscript means first "determin[ing] what words the
  characters of the manuscript stand for" — the graphs underdetermine the words; that
  determination is the reader's act.
- **Galambos** (*Orthography of Early Chinese Writing*, 2006) *(background for the detail; thesis
  confirmed via secondary sources)*: variant forms in Warring States manuscripts were **normal,
  accepted orthographic flexibility** — not errors against a standard, because there was no
  standard to err against. The very concept of a single "correct" form of each character is the
  later, Han-consolidated idea, read backwards.
- **Richter** ("Towards a Profile of Graphic Variation," on the Mawangdui Laozi manuscripts):
  variant distribution can be *profiled* — it patterns, rather than scattering like noise.
- The Guodian Laozi itself writes 難 "difficult" six ways, differing by component presence
  — within one manuscript, uncorrected, evidently unproblematic.
- **Kern** (composite texts, writing and orality): early texts were "composite, authorless
  artifacts" — repertoires realized in performance and memory, not fixed originals passed down a
  copy chain. Under this model the stemmatic picture (an urtext accumulating one copyist's errors
  at a time) **does not describe the period**. Variation between witnesses is mostly *different
  realizations*, not *degraded copies*.

This is the scholarly footing for the "shorthand" intuition, in its defensible form: a Chu graph
was a **pointer whose resolution was supplied in use** — by context, by the reading tradition, by
whoever was doing the teaching. The same token could be resolved by different readers into
different words (the 亙/恆/極 nexus in our own Bundle A survey is a live example). The
consolidation replaced resolutive reading with fixed reading: **one graph, one word, one form**.
What disappeared was not ink but latitude.

## 4. The intentionality guard

What cannot be claimed: that the scribe (or a lesson-tradition behind him) *designed* the graph's
openness as pedagogy — "shorthand meant to open a lesson's worth of meaning." No source attests
scribal intent; there is still no witness for a reconstructed stage. The removal of the etymology
guard in v9.3 did not remove that fact.

What can be claimed, on the record above:

1. The graph underdetermined the word (Boltz — attested practice, not speculation).
2. Reading was therefore resolutive — an act performed with the text, plausibly *taught* with it
   (the Guodian strips are a tomb tutor's copy; instruction is the likeliest use-context, but
   this is inference, and should be marked as such).
3. Consolidation fixed the resolution and libian destroyed the components — two distinct
   flattenings, both systemic, neither accidental.
4. Therefore the received text is not a *worn* copy of the Chu text; it is a **resolved and
   re-clothed** one: each open token forced to a single word, each word re-dressed in a graph
   that no longer shows its structure.

Point 4 is the load-bearing reframe, and it needs nothing stronger than points 1–3.

## 5. The mechanisms actually documented for our specific cases

| Chain-relevant change | Documented mechanism | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 恆→常 (four instances, post-Mawangdui) | Naming taboo for Emperor Wen (Liu Heng, r. 179–157) — later editions "uniformly changed" them | **Policy edit** — deliberate, dated, total |
| 邦→國 | Naming taboo for Liu Bang — already visible *between* MWD A and B | **Policy edit** — and it shows the mechanism operating inside our witness window |
| 也-stripping (Guodian → MWD → received) | SEP: the excavated texts are "syntactically clearer… thanks to the larger number of grammatical particles"; the received text is the regularized one. *(background)* The regularization runs toward four-beat verse and quotable-classic form — classicization, with editorial fixing (Liu Xiang's project collated editions and excised at will, 26 BCE) | **Stylistic/editorial regularization** — directional, not random |
| Graph substitutions (戁→難, 亙→恆/常, 又亡→有無, 先→前 …) | Orthographic fixing under the Qin-through-Han consolidation: one word per graph, standard form per word | **Systematization** |
| Component illegibility | 隸變 | **Script transformation** |
| Local token variants (e.g. a dropped repeated 有) | Haplography/eye-skip — real, but case-by-case | **Scribal error — the residual class** |

Five systemic mechanisms, one residual accident class. That is the inversion of the weighting the
chain's current prose carries.

## Addendum 2026-08-05 — adjudicating the parallel assessment (project-chat instance)

Will ran the same prompt in the project chat; a second Claude instance answered. Convergences
priced near zero per the ledger rule. Three divergences, adjudicated:

**1. Single-boundary vs. staircase (substantive — the parallel account is wrong here).** The
project-chat instance re-frames the alternative to drift as "a systematizing project… in one or
two political events," and proposes the Mawangdui test accordingly: boundary-clustering predicts
a large Guodian→MWD jump and a small MWD→received one. But the documented changes already refuse
a single boundary: the script is already clerical by MWD, 邦→國 flips *between* MWD A and B,
恆→常 comes *after* MWD (that is why MWD preserves 恆 at all), the 也-stripping continues after
MWD, and Liu Xiang's collation is 26 BCE. The record is a **staircase of discrete deliberate
events spread over ~3 centuries** — serial fixation, not one boundary and not drift. As designed,
the two-model test would mis-score: MWD *will* sit intermediate, which the stated dichotomy reads
as evidence for drift. The facsimile-errand upgrade survives and improves: score it for a
staircase — date **each change class separately** (graph forms / taboo pairs / particles /
ordering) and the mechanism history falls out. That is a stronger result than a tie-break.

**2. The Qin event's reality (the parallel account leans on the narrative the paleography
challenges).** It asserts 書同文 as an effective 221 BCE event and the regional scripts as
"genuinely divergent" systems — the traditional picture Galambos 2004/2006 specifically argues is
an Eastern-Han retrospective, with variation pervasive *within* regions and no sudden shift in
the archaeology. Notably, the parallel account applies the retrospection critique to the Shuowen
(correctly: Xu Shen retro-analyzing seal forms he took for original structure) while accepting
the Shiji/Hanshu unification story — Han sources of the same retrospective stratum. Inconsistent
skepticism; the defensible phrasing remains Qin-*through*-Han.

**3. The closing identity ("the script reform is your π dichotomy, applied to writing").**
Asserted as a payoff. Attractor-shaped: find a matching set, declare an identity, exactly where
it flatters the framework — and in identity grammar ("is… applied to"), not `::`. Both instances
reached for an RSM mapping (this report's §6 closing note is the same shape) — that convergence
is priced near zero; the divergence is that one instance asserted it and one flagged and
distrusted it. **Ruled 2026-08-05: rejected by Will; logged as divergence ledger Entry 005** (checked against
the `::` grammar and the claimed shared invariant, which fails — deferred exactness vs. enforced
exactness; see the entry).

**What was true inside it (always stated):** the accumulation/boundary distinction is a real
sharpening of the question; the MWD test is genuinely good once re-scored for a staircase; the
溺-read-as-弱 case as *resolution* (a standardizer choosing the lexicon-available word) is a
strong candidate framing for the 溺 checksum item; and its census of erosion language in the
papers (Ch. 2, Ch. 40, Ch. 25 "adds hierarchy") complements this report's chain-side list in §6 —
the two scopes together cover the corpus.
