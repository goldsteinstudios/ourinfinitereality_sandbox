## VII. The path

Draw the locked pair as a curve — form against void, product constant — and read off four structural facts. (The drawing is a chart; every fact below has been checked to survive redrawing, per the first test of Section X.)

**The curve is the connection.** The two modes' extremes — form-dominant and void-dominant — are joined by one connected arc, traversable continuously, and the arc bends around the center without ever touching it. For a long stretch of this project, the connection between the modes seemed to require an added structure: a circle appended to a broken axis, a rotation postulated to carry one mode to the other. The Q1 ruling dissolved the need. The broken axis — two rays severed by an excluded midpoint — was an artifact of the signed chart, which placed the unoccupiable pivot on the path. In magnitudes, the path was never broken: the relation's own curve is the around-route, and "no through, only around" is not a principle the geometry must be made to satisfy but a plain description of the curve. The traversal debt, long carried, is paid — not by an addition, but by a correction of the drawing.

**There are two pivots, not one.** The balance — where form equals void — lies *on* the curve and is fully reachable, because both modes are present there in equal amount, and equal amounts of two things is not the two things becoming one. The center — where both modes would vanish — lies *off* the curve and is never reached. The signed chart had collapsed these into a single point and inherited a contradiction: the pivot that organizes the gradient seemed both necessary and impossible to stand on. In magnitudes they come apart. Balance is occupiable; the center is not; and the framework's practical arm — harmony as an achievable placement, the vessel as an ordinary object — depends on exactly this. If balance were unoccupiable, the Dao De Jing's entire counsel would describe something no one could do.

**Scale is position on the curve.** Multiply form by any factor and divide void by the same factor: the product — reality — is untouched, and the operation slides the point along the curve. The curve is therefore the complete set of rescalings of one reality, and the mathematical name for such a set, chosen by people who never heard of this framework, is *the orbit*. Scale hereby receives a precise, frame-internal meaning: zooming is motion along the curve, reality-preserving and reversible, and it is *not* the recursion. The two had been muddled for years of drafts — "zoom in far enough and you reach the next frame" — and the muddle is now resolved by assignment: rescaling is within-frame; the recursion, Section VIII, is something else. One further consequence falls out: since rescaling maps the curve to itself and the curve never meets the center, no rescaling reaches the center. The pivot is not merely unoccupied but invariantly unoccupied, at every scale — which is what the older formulation, "zoom in and you are always on one side," was reaching for.

**The asymptotes are the impossibility, drawn.** The curve's two unreachable limits — all form and no void, all void and no form — are the saturation face and the absence face of P₀. That the curve touches neither is the earlier sections rendered visible: everything that obtains carries both modes, and nothing actual is pure solid or pure emptiness.
