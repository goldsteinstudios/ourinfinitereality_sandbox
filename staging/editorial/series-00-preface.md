# Around the Center — The Essays
## Series preface and index (v7.7, r1)

This series takes one concept at a time and maps it across the frameworks the RSM works in — the structural derivation, the mathematics, the physics correspondences, and the Guodian text — now that each framework is reasonably solid in its own lane. Every essay follows one rule: **the invariant is named first**, in plain language, and then each register points at it. The registers never merge. A correspondence written `::` means *maps to, never is* — two expressions from different languages aiming at one structural fact, each distorting it in its own way.

Claims carry their prices. **[derived]** means it follows from the stated premises and has been checked. **[candidate]** means a promising argument with a named gap. **[P2]** marks a physics correspondence found after the formal result existed — a rendering, carrying no evidential weight of its own. **[G]** marks text attested on the Guodian strips (~300 BCE); **[R]** marks text known only from the received tradition, centuries younger. Where an essay leans on results resolved in the most recent working cycle, those results are additionally gated on the author's adversarial read and say so.

Some essays are expositions: they show the map. Others are refusal stories: a register said no to its authors, and the correction is the content. The refusal essays — *On Energy* most of all — are the ones doing evidential work, because under single authorship, agreement is cheap and refusal is expensive. One essay is a fourth kind: *What Pi Names* opens the curing room, where the project's most beautiful observations are held at observation grade until they earn derivations or deflate — the discipline of not-yet, displayed rather than described.

One essay is deliberately absent. A probabilistic register was proposed, developed, and killed by ruling during the v7.7 cycle. Per the project's survivor-only discipline, the essay is not written, and this sentence is the only record the series keeps.

**Reading order.** The series opens where the framework is most certain and closes by handing the reader a genuinely open problem — that arc is the project's honesty argument in miniature.
